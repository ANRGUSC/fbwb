FLYBRAIN TO WEBOTS: WHAT CHANGED AND WHY

The original evolutionary-behavior source is retained unchanged in
vendor/flybrain (commit 97e02fb7c8d19fd5267c3fa9194f8001e08fb613).
The changes below are in our adapter and the separate "corrected" model.

1. Simulator interface. Replaced the live Tello camera/command interface with
Webots camera frames and a Crazyflie velocity/altitude PID. Converted forward
commands to m/s and reversed the yaw sign for Webots' coordinate convention.
This connects the brain's abstract commands to a simulated physical drone.

2. Neural timing and units. The original dynamics were numerically unstable.
Added exponential membrane/synapse integration, explicit voltage-scale synaptic
increments and signed synapse-count weights, plus delayed events and refractory
handling. Each 20 ms physics step now receives exactly twenty 1 ms neural steps;
simulation waits for inference. This avoids numerical blow-up and clock drift.
Stabilizing the original integrator alone left the model effectively silent;
the unit/gain changes are therefore a separate model revision, not just a
faster or smaller timestep.
The older original and stability-only modes remain available for comparison.

3. Sensory encoding. Adapted image conversion and optical flow to Webots; added
a small motion noise floor so stationary frames do not become maximum input
after normalization. Optical-flow features drive seeded Poisson activation of
receptor cells. This supplies explicit input rates and reproducible trials.

4. Bidirectional turning. Replaced arbitrary halves of the neuron list with
FlyWire anatomical side annotations, joined by exact neuron ID. Left/right
image features now drive the corresponding R1-6 groups. Turning compares mean
motion-cell firing rates, with side gains measured using symmetric stimulation.
This removes pool-size and response-gain biases. Left/right stimuli validate
the decoder but are not used to fit the gains. Yaw comes from actual network
spikes, not a scripted turn or a direct camera-to-motor bypass.

5. Flight stability. Added command smoothing, slew limits and a controlled
takeoff. Preserved PID state and running rotors during held trials so release
does not cause a fall. Altitude remains under PID control; neural climb is
shown but not applied. Sideways is disabled; the forward decoder cannot reverse.

6. Explainability. Added measured receptor/network/output telemetry, a live
panel, synchronized replay and printed narration. Structured visual probes
precede actual neural left/right turns on a yaw-only rig, then free flight
using the onboard camera. The turn rig uses a stabilized test camera to
separate stimulus effects from motion of the drone's own camera.

Data: reconstructed the graph from official public v783 exports because the
author's private processed CSVs were unavailable. Sparse computation keeps
all 139,255 neurons in use on this CPU; there is no neuron subsampling.

Scope: anatomical side means soma side, not an ommatidial visual-field
map. The gain-balanced turn readout is an engineering interface, not a claim
to reproduce a biological steering circuit. Physiological validity remains
limited, including GABA-only inhibition, very negative voltages and persistent
recurrent activity.
Successful flight establishes integration behavior, not biological validation.

Measured validation: 19 tests pass. The neural turn trials rotate the drone
48.6 degrees left and 47.1 degrees right. It then completes six seconds of
onboard-camera free flight, travelling 0.294 m with no airborne collisions;
final altitude is 0.995 m. The left/right trials use a yaw rig; the final
segment releases the fixture and enables neural forward motion as well.
