WATCHING THE FLYBRAIN DEMO

Double-click Run Explained Demo.cmd. Webots appears on the left and the
measurement panel on the right. Eighteen simulated seconds take several minutes
on this CPU: each camera frame waits for its full neural computation.

0-6 s: TAKEOFF. The ordinary altitude PID raises the drone to one metre.
This movement is not a neural decision.

6.0-6.4 s: STILL BASELINE. The simulator holds the pose. Static patterns
should produce zero visual drive and no spikes in the reset network.
The altitude controller and rotors remain running throughout the fixture.

6.4-6.8 s: LEFT SCREEN. The blue screen moves; the left-image feature rises.
Watch the receptor target rates, individual measured spike rates, then
motion-pool and descending-neuron activity. Neural commands are shown but
forward/yaw commands are withheld while the pose is held.

6.8-7.2 s: RIGHT SCREEN. The orange screen moves instead. Brain state and
camera history are reset at each held trial, with the same random seed,
so prior trial activity does not contaminate the comparison.

7.2-7.6 s: OUTWARD MOTION. Both patterns move away from the image centre.
Watch the radial feature and the forward decoder. These are visual probes,
not obstacles that the controller understands semantically.

7.6-8.0 s: VERTICAL MOTION. Patterns move vertically. Inspect the vertical
feature and R7/R8 proxy drive. Climb output is displayed but not connected
to flight; altitude still belongs to the PID.

8-10 s: LEFT TURN. A yaw-only rig holds position while neural yaw drives the
rotors. A stabilized test camera sees the left moving pattern. Watch the
positive applied yaw and the positive measured heading. The yaw is decoded
from measured neural activity; no scripted turn command is inserted.

10-12 s: RIGHT TURN. Pose and brain reset; now the right pattern moves. Watch
negative applied yaw and negative measured heading. Raw anatomical left/right
rates are divided by reference rates measured under symmetric stimulation,
so neither pool wins merely because its baseline gain is larger.

12-18 s: FREE FLIGHT. The rig releases from a centred pose and the brain resets.
The onboard camera replaces the stabilized test camera. The left pattern moves;
neural forward/yaw outputs reach the flight controller. Subsequent turns are
determined by camera feedback and network activity, not a scheduled direction.
Compare targets with measured motion: filtering, inertia and altitude recovery
cause lag. Persistent activity can continue after visual features change.

READING THE PANEL

Each receptor square is a real sampled receptor neuron, not an anatomical
ommatidium. Hover for its FlyWire ID, observed Hz, requested drive Hz and
input-event count. All rates/counts use a 20 ms simulated window. Colour
brightness saturates at 300 Hz; numerical readouts retain the actual rate.
Drive events and actual spikes differ because the network also influences
the cells. Population means use all cells in their labelled group.

The source adapter supplies only four image-wide optical-flow features.
Corrected mode now uses anatomical side labels for R1-6 and motion neurons.
These are soma-side labels, not a complete ommatidial visual-field map.
Ratios cross-activate: horizontal motion can activate the vertical ratio,
and vertical patterns can yield horizontal flow. Read the measured bars;
do not assume each screen stimulates only one biological pathway.

Forward is nonnegative in this decoder: it cannot command reverse. Turn
uses the gain-balanced mean rates of anatomical motion-cell pools. Webots positive
yaw turns left, so the adapter reverses the source's Tello yaw convention.
Sideways is disabled. Neural climb is shown but unused. Motor commands
are signed rotor speeds, not separate neural motor-neuron outputs.

The printed narrative explains these measured decoder and controller
relationships; it is not a neuron-by-neuron causal proof. This model's
physiology is experimental, including very negative membrane voltages.

AFTER THE RUN

The finite simulation closes Webots. The panel stays open. Click Replay
to play the recording, then use the slider to inspect a particular frame.
The phase dropdown jumps straight to LEFT TURN, RIGHT TURN or FREE FLIGHT.
If necessary click Load phases first; it loads the available recording.
Pause view freezes only the panel. Stop run requests an orderly simulator
stop after its current computation. Replay Explained Demo.cmd reopens
the last recording. Printed explanations are also saved in
results/explained-bilateral/narrative.txt, alongside live.ndjson and telemetry.csv.
